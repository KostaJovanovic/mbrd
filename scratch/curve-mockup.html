Softish curves — how much should a line bow?

Two scenes per panel. Top: the cards sit at different heights (where a curve's character actually shows). Bottom: a card sits directly in the path and the line arches over it. Dashed grey is the direct path for reference; brown is the real connection. When two cards are level and nothing is between them, every option draws a straight line.

1 · Taut

Short, controlled bends. The line takes the most direct clean route and only curves as much as it must. Tidy on a busy board.

2 · Relaxed

A smooth, easy S between offset cards and a comfortable arch over an obstacle. Reads as a natural cable without drawing attention.

3 · Noodle

Long, sweeping handles like the cables in a node editor. Very organic and playful, but the loops grow large and can look busy when a board has many lines.

The other two tiers

Softish is locked (Relaxed offset S, gentle arch). These two are orthogonal — straight runs that turn at right angles. Middle rounds the corners; Harsh keeps them sharp and snapped to the board grid. Same two scenes as above.

Middle · rounded corners

Straight horizontal and vertical runs, turning at softly rounded corners. Reads as a tidy flowchart wire.

Harsh · sharp, snapped

The same orthogonal runs, but every corner is a hard 90 degrees and the path snaps to the board grid. Strict and technical.
