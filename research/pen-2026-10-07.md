# The pen — 7 October 2026

**Status: done, 2026-10-08.** Built as designed, with the differences in the
last section. Touch and a real stylus are untested on a device.

Item 17 of `features-2026-10-07.md`. Decided there and not reopened here: a
freehand pen, an eraser and a highlighter; the strokes follow the whimsy dial;
a burst of strokes (no pause over about two seconds, close together) is one
drawing card. No shapes and no text tool.

## What somebody sees

A **Draw** button joins the toolbar between Link and Stickers, `<button
data-cmd="draw">` like the rest, and `D` toggles it. Pressing it puts the board
in drawing mode: the button stays lit, the cursor is a nib, and a small bar
under the toolbar holds the three tools (pen, highlighter, eraser), five inks
and three widths. `Esc`, `D` again, or the button leaves.

While the mode is on:

- **A mouse or a stylus draws.** A finger draws as well on a phone, and two
  fingers still pan and pinch. Space and the middle button still pan, as they
  do everywhere.
- **Each stroke lands at once.** Strokes laid down in a burst gather into one
  card: a new stroke joins the drawing being made when it starts within 2 s of
  the last stroke's end *and* within reach of that drawing (its bounds grown by
  a quarter of their longer side, at least 96 world units). Otherwise it starts
  a new drawing card. Pausing to look, then adding an arrow next to the sketch,
  starts a new card, which is right: it is a second mark.
- **Leaving the mode** settles the last drawing. From then on it is a card like
  any other: it moves, resizes (the strokes scale with it), turns, sits in a
  fence, joins, and goes to the bin.

Outside the mode a drawing is never added to. Drawing on top of an old sketch
makes a new card over it. Rejoining one is a later question (see the end).

## The three tools, and the dial

| | Softish (0) | Middle (1) | Harsh (2) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| pen | ink with a nib: width follows pressure and speed, ends taper, a faint wobble | smooth, width follows pressure only, round ends | one width, no pressure, square ends, corners kept sharp |
| highlighter | soft-edged, multiplied, a little uneven | flat, multiplied | flat rectangle-ended band, multiplied |
| inks | the board's own: `--ink`, `--accent`, `--accent-warm`, `--leafy`, `--danger` | same | same |

The inks are **tokens, stored by name** (`ink`, `accent`, `warm`, `leaf`,
`danger`), never as hex. A drawing redraws in the board's palette when the
look changes, and in colourblind mode it follows the remapped tokens. That is
the same choice connections make with `meta.color`.

**The dial changes how a stroke is drawn, never what was stored.** The points
and pressures are kept as captured, and the tier decides the outline at draw
time. A sketch made at Softish and viewed at Harsh is the same sketch, drawn
plainly.

The **eraser** removes whole strokes: whatever stroke the eraser's path
crosses, in the drawing under it, goes. Partial erasing (cutting a stroke in
two) is a second implementation of hit-testing against an outline, for
something people do far less often than they undo. Undo is one stroke at a
time, whichever tool made it. Erasing the last stroke of a drawing removes
the card.

## The card

A new item type, **`drawing`**:

```json
{
  "id": "d81k2m", "type": "drawing",
  "x": 120, "y": -40, "w": 300, "h": 180, "rot": 0, "z": 14,
  "name": "",
  "meta": {
    "strokes": [
      { "tool": "pen", "ink": "accent", "width": 3, "pts": "…" },
      { "tool": "highlighter", "ink": "warm", "width": 18, "pts": "…" }
    ],
    "space": [300, 180]
  }
}
```

- `pts` is the points in the card's own space (`space`, its size when drawn,
  so a resized card scales its strokes rather than reflowing them), as one
  string of base-36 integers `x,y,p` at a tenth of a unit. Compact, legible
  enough to see it is a list of numbers, and with no binary in `board.json`.
- **Simplified when the stroke ends** (Ramer-Douglas-Peucker at a quarter of
  a unit), and **ceilinged**: at most 2000 points a stroke and 400 strokes a
  card. A stroke past the first ceiling is simplified harder. Past the second,
  a new card starts. These are plain caps on what the app writes, not
  consent ceilings: nothing here reads a file somebody else wrote.
- `name` is empty; the card's accessible name is "Drawing, 6 strokes". A
  drawing can be renamed like any card, and the name is what Find matches.
- **The schema change.** A new `type` and two new `meta` keys. That costs
  nothing under the format's own rules: an older build draws an unknown type
  as a plain named card and carries `meta` through untouched. No `requires`
  entry, no version change. `mbrd-format.md` gets a `drawing` row in "meta, by
  type".

## How it is built, where

- **Input: inside `canvas/input.ts`, as one more gesture.** A `stroke`
  gesture, entered from `press` when drawing mode is on, with its row in
  `GESTURE_MOVES`. The single pipeline is the rule here (CLAUDE.md: "Do not
  split canvas/input.ts"), and a pen with its own listeners is exactly the
  second pipeline it forbids. Pointer events give pressure (`e.pressure`, 0.5
  for a mouse) and coalesced points (`getCoalescedEvents()`), so a fast stroke
  is not a polygon.
- **While a stroke is drawn** it is a single SVG path in a layer over the
  world, redrawn per frame from the points. Only on pointer-up does it become
  board state, one `commit()`, so a stroke costs one undo step and one autosave.
- **The outline: `canvas/strokes.ts`, pure.** Points and pressures in, an SVG
  path `d` out, per tier: the variable-width outline (the method perfect-
  freehand popularised, written here rather than imported, like everything
  else in the app), the round- or square-ended band, the wobble seeded by the
  stroke's own points so it does not shimmer between redraws. Tested under
  `node --test` like `web-route.ts`.
- **The renderer: one entry in `RENDERERS`** plus its branch in `classify()`
  (never for a file, so the branch is only the type). It draws an `<svg>` with
  one `<path>` a stroke, filled with `var(--ink)` and so on, highlighters first
  with `mix-blend-mode: multiply`. Because it is DOM, the export captures it
  with no painter (ui/capture.ts). For Safari, `snapshot.ts` gets a
  `drawing` painter that strokes the same `d` strings through `Path2D`.
- **The command:** `draw` in `cmds`, the button in `index.html`, the icon a
  `<symbol>` in `icons.svg`, the tool bar a `FLYOUTS` entry or an
  `openAnchored()` panel, never a second menu. The tool, ink and width are
  remembered per device in prefs, not per board.
- **JSON Canvas export** writes a drawing as a file node holding an `.svg` of
  it. **Arrangements** treat it as any card. **Find** matches its name only.

## Order of work

1. `canvas/strokes.ts` and its tests: points to outline for each tier,
   simplification, the burst rule as a pure function (`joinsDrawing(last, at,
   time)`).
2. The `drawing` type: schema normalisation, the renderer, the painter, the
   accessible name, the `mbrd-format.md` row.
3. The `stroke` gesture in `input.ts`, the mode, the command, the button and
   the tool bar, the pen only.
4. Highlighter, eraser, inks, widths.
5. Touch: one finger draws while the mode is on, two pan, palm rejection by
   ignoring touches while a stylus is down.

Each lands checked in a browser at all three tiers: the drawing on the board,
in an export, and after save, refresh and recover.

## Questions for the maintainer

Answered on 2026-10-08: the recommendation, in all four. A stroke that starts
on a drawing card joins it, the default ink is `ink`, the key is `D`, and a
drawing stays level.

1. **Rejoining an old drawing.** As designed, a stroke never joins a settled
   card. The alternative is: a stroke that starts *on* a drawing card while
   the mode is on joins it, whatever the time. It's more useful and slightly
   less predictable. Recommended: yes, join on start-over-card.
2. **Default ink.** `ink` (the board's own text colour), or the accent?
   Recommended: `ink`.
3. **The `D` key** is free today. Confirm it, or name another.
4. **A drawing's lean.** Cards lean at Softish. A drawing could lean like a
   card, or stay level the way fences do. Recommended: level. A sketch
   annotating something beside it should not drift off it.

## What was built, and where it differs

Built on 2026-10-08: `canvas/strokes.ts` (the arithmetic, tested in
`tests/strokes.test.js`), `canvas/drawing.ts` (the card's face),
`canvas/pen.ts` (the mode, the live stroke, where a stroke lands), the `stroke`
gesture in `canvas/input.ts`, `ui/pen-bar.ts` and `#pen-bar`, `setDrawings()`
in `state.ts`, the painter in `ui/snapshot.ts`, and the JSON Canvas export's
`.svg` of a drawing. Checked headless in Chromium: drawing, the burst rule,
joining a drawing by starting on it, highlighter, eraser, undo, the three tiers,
Escape, save and reload, and the SVG export.

Where it differs from the design above:

- **Softish follows pressure, not speed.** The stored points carry no time, and
  once simplified their spacing says how straight the line was rather than how
  fast it was drawn, so a speed reading would have been made up. Softish tapers
  its ends and wobbles; Middle follows pressure; Harsh is one width.
- **The two plain tiers stroke a centre line** rather than fill an outline:
  Harsh's pen and every highlighter are one width, which a stroked path says
  exactly. Softish and Middle fill an outline, as designed.
- **Sizes are screen pixels.** A stroke is as wide on screen as the size says
  at whatever zoom it is drawn, and stored at that width in the world.
- **A drawing does not snap to the lattice** when it lands, and is never
  smaller than the smallest card (48 units): its space is padded round a small
  mark, so the mark is drawn where it was put and at its own size.
- **The card draws its space at one scale, centred** (`viewBox` with `meet`):
  a resized or stretched card shows the sketch whole, never squashed.
- **Draw is behind More on a narrow toolbar**, beside Colour, Link and
  Stickers, and refuses on a Mobile-layout board as Join does. Taking up the pen
  puts the Join tool down and the other way round.
- **An eraser pass is one undo step**, and a drawing it empties goes to the bin
  in a second step.
