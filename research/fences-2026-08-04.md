# Fences — regions that hold the cards inside them

Open. Nothing below is built.

A **fence** is a labelled rectangle drawn on the board. The cards inside it
belong to it: drag the fence and they come along, name the fence and you have
named that part of the board. It is the idea Stardock's Fences puts on a Windows
desktop, and it is the answer to the thing an infinite canvas is worst at —
a board that has grown past the point where "over there, near the blue one" is a
usable address.

This document is the reasoning. It is not a specification and it is not a
promise; the two decisions marked **open** below are the maintainer's, and one of
them changes the size of the work by a factor of three.

---

## What a fence is, precisely

| | |
| --- | --- |
| a fence *is* | a rectangle, a name, and a look |
| a fence *has* | whatever cards are inside it, right now |
| a fence *does* | carry those cards when it moves, and give them one label |
| a fence is **not** | a folder, a container that owns its contents, or a second board |

The distinction in that last row is the whole design. A folder holds things —
take a thing out and you have changed the folder. A fence *covers* things: it
holds whatever happens to be under it, and the way to take something out is to
drag it out. There is no add, no remove, no membership list, and nothing that can
disagree with the picture on screen.

That is not a novel idea in this codebase. It is exactly what `sticky.js` already
does for notes, in as many words:

> stuckness is a fact about where two things are, and a file that also recorded
> it could disagree with its own geometry — a hazard with no upside, since the
> geometry is right there and the answer falls out of it.

A fence is that sentence with a different predicate. Most of the plan below is
the consequences of taking it seriously.

---

## Decision 1 — a fence is an item

Two ways to store one, and the format decides it.

**Rejected: a top-level list**, `board.fences: [{ id, name, rect }]`, filed
beside `connections`. It reads cleaner — a fence is not content, so why is it in
`items`? — and it is wrong for one concrete reason, which `docs/mbrd-format.md`
already states about `connections`:

> An older reader … does **not** write it back, because `board.json` is rebuilt
> field by field rather than carried through. So a board saved by a build that
> predates this loses its connections. That is the one place the format's
> forward-compatibility promise does not reach, and **it is worth knowing before
> adding another top-level key.**

That paragraph was written as a warning to whoever came next. This is whoever
came next. A board with fences, opened and saved by any build older than the one
that adds them, would come back with the fences gone — silently, and with the
cards scattered where the fence used to hold them.

**Chosen: `type: "fence"` in `board.items`.** The same document says what happens
then:

> An unknown `type` — a reader that does not recognise one shows the item as a
> plain named card rather than dropping it, and writes it back out untouched.

So an old build shows a fence as a large empty named card, in the right place, at
the right size, and hands it back intact. That is a graceful degradation of a
new feature rather than the destruction of somebody's work, and it is the same
extension point `swatch` was added through.

Everything else follows from the choice, and it is a lot of free machinery:

| a fence gets, for nothing | because |
| --- | --- |
| independent Desktop and Mobile geometry | `board.layouts` is keyed by item id |
| a name you can edit in place | `editItemName()` in `canvas/items.js` |
| selection, delete, undo, bin, restore | `state.js` treats it as an item |
| z-order, resize grips, the context menu | ditto |
| culling | `canvas/spatial.js` indexes any box |
| save and load | `storage/mbrd.js` writes items generically |

The cost is the mirror image: a fence is a card, and every place that means
"something the user put on the board as content" now has one more thing to
exclude. That list is enumerated under **What has to change** below, and it is
the real work.

## Decision 2 — membership is derived, memoised, and seeded

**A card is in a fence when its centre is inside the fence's rectangle.**

Centre, not overlap fraction, and the difference matters. `sticky.js` measures a
note by area because a sticky pressed onto the *corner* of a photograph is
obviously stuck to it. A fence is the opposite case: it is a region, regions have
edges, and a card straddling the edge has to be in exactly one of the two sides.
The centre answers that with no threshold to tune and no card ever in two fences
at once.

Where fences nest, the **smallest** containing fence wins. A fence inside a fence
is then a sub-region, which is the only thing it could sensibly be, and the
containment chain terminates because each step is strictly smaller.

### Memoised, on exactly `sticky.js`'s terms

Measured once, then remembered until the card itself is handled. This is not an
optimisation; it is the behaviour. Without it, dragging a fence across the board
would hoover up every loose card it passed over, and a card dropped near a fence
would join it a frame later when something else moved. `sticky.js` names the
rule — "only picking up the note itself asks the question again" — and
`commitGeom(label, before, driven)` already implements it: the `driven` ids are
what the gesture actually had hold of, and only those are re-measured.

### One place the rule differs, deliberately

`sticky.js` says resizing a host cannot re-parent the notes on it. **A fence must
re-measure on resize.** The two are not inconsistent: a photograph's edges are
incidental to what is lying on it, and a fence's edges *are* the fence. Dragging
a fence's corner out over three more cards is the gesture that means "and these
too"; there is no other one, and requiring the user to then touch each card would
make the boundary decorative.

So: **moving a fence does not re-measure; resizing one does.** Both halves are
testable and both should be tested, because each is surprising if you only know
the other.

### Seeded from the file

`meta.fence` on the *member*, holding the fence's id or `null` — the same shape
and the same job as `meta.stuckTo`, stamped from live geometry at serialize time
and read back by a `seedFences()` beside `seedSticks()`.

It exists for the same narrow reason: a pixel of drift across save and reload, or
a layout that parked a card a hair outside, must not lose a grouping somebody
plainly made. It is a durable *record*, never the authority — a card whose `meta`
names a fence that is not on the board falls through to measuring, exactly as a
dangling `stuckTo` does. Absent on an older board, which then measures, which is
correct.

Storing it on the member rather than a list on the fence is what keeps the format
honest: it is one `meta` key, additive, carried through untouched by older
readers, and there is no second place for it to disagree with the geometry.

## What travels, and where that is decided

The whole "what comes along when this moves" surface is **four call sites**, and
they are already there for sticky notes:

| site | today |
| --- | --- |
| `canvas/input.js:634` | `[...selection, ...stuckFollowers(selection)]` — drag a selection |
| `canvas/input.js:720` | `stuckFollowers([id])` — drag one card |
| `canvas/input.js:1519` | `stuckFollowers(selection)` — arrow-key nudge |
| `layout.js:623` | `if (driven) restick(driven)` — inside `commitGeom` |

A fence adds `fenceFollowers()` and `refence()` at those same four, and nowhere
else. The narrowness of that seam is the strongest evidence the model is right —
if a fifth site turned up, the design would be wrong.

`stuckFollowers()` already handles the case that would otherwise bite: *"Anything
already moving is left out"*, so a marquee that catches a fence and its members
moves each card once rather than once as a selection and again as a follower.
`fenceFollowers()` must keep that property, and the two must be unioned before
either is asked — a note stuck to a card inside a fence has to travel with the
fence, which means the fixed point of both relations, not one then the other.

**Where the two genuinely merge is z-order.** `stackRoot()` in `stacking.js`
walks a card up to the bottom-most thing that owns its layer, and it currently
knows only about sticks. A fence sits behind its members, so "bring this fence to
front" has to carry them, and `stackRoot()` has to walk fence membership too.
That is the one function where fences and stickies stop being parallel and become
one question.

## The interior is transparent

A fence is a large card, and the first thing a large card breaks is that
`left-drag empty space` pans the board. Inside a 2000-unit fence there would be
no empty space left.

So the fence's body is `pointer-events: none`. What takes a press is the **label
bar** along its top edge and a few pixels of **border**:

```
┌─ kitchen ──────────────────────┐   ← bar and border: drag, resize, select
│                                │
│    ▢        ▢                  │   ← interior: presses fall through to the
│                                │      board, exactly as before
│         ▢                      │
└────────────────────────────────┘
```

Pan works inside a fence. Marquee works inside a fence. A card inside a fence is
hit before the fence, because the fence's body cannot be hit at all. And "drag
the fence" is one unambiguous target rather than a guess about whether the press
landed on a card.

This also settles the resize grips: they are on the border, which is where the
existing grip machinery already puts them, so nothing special is needed.

## The open question — Mobile

**This is the decision that changes the size of the work, and it is not mine to
make.**

Mobile is a single column, six or eight grid spaces wide, packed first-fit by
`placeMobileItems()`. `layouts.mobile.arrangement` *names an order, not a shape*
— the format document is explicit about it. A fence is a two-dimensional region,
and a column has no two-dimensional regions in it.

Three answers, and they are not close together:

### (a) Fences are Desktop furniture — recommended for a first version

A fence has no Mobile geometry, is never packed, and is never drawn there. This
is precisely what the Desktop title card already does: `completeLayout()` parks
it clear of the Mobile board, `placeMobileItems()` filters it out of the
obstacles, and `canvas/mobile-frame.js` draws the phone's masthead instead. The
pattern is proven and the code to copy is three lines.

What the user gets on a phone: the cards, in the column, in the order the
arrangement chose — which is exactly what they get today. Nothing is lost that
they had.

What it costs them: the grouping they made on a desktop is invisible on a phone.
That is a real gap and it should be stated in the release notes rather than
discovered.

### (b) A fence becomes a run in the column, with a band label

The pack orders by fence, so a fence's members come out contiguous, with the
fence drawn as a full-width label band above them. This is the *right* answer —
it is the honest translation of a region into an order, and it is what
`arrangement` already means on Mobile.

It is also a second project. It changes `packMobileGrid()` from "first-fit each
item" to "first-fit each item, within a run", it needs a new full-width cell
shape for the band, it interacts with `mobileColumnCount` and `spacing`, and it
puts a sort key in front of an arrangement that currently owns the order
outright. Estimate it separately; do not fold it into a first version.

### (c) A fence packs as a card with its members riding it

`attachRiders()` exists and would do it. It is the wrong answer: `fitMobile()`
scales every card to the column width, so a fence would arrive on the phone at
column width with a Desktop-sized interior, and either its members ride to
positions outside it or the fence keeps a size nothing else on that board is
allowed to keep. The mechanism fits; the result does not.

**Recommendation: (a) now, (b) as its own document later.** Ship the feature on
the layout it was designed for, and do not let the phone's column dictate the
desktop's regions.

## What has to change

Grouped by why, not by directory. Line counts are estimates.

### New files (2)

| file | ~lines | what |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `web/assets/js/fences.js` | 200 | the base-layer relation: `fenceOf`, `fenceFollowers`, `refence`, `seedFences`, `forgetFences`, `fencePlacement`. Pure, no DOM, no `state` import — beside `sticky.js` |
| `web/assets/js/canvas/fence.js` | 150 | the card: label bar, border, the roll-up toggle if it lands |

Both need adding to `SHELL` in `web/sw.js` — `tests/sw.test.js` walks
`assets/js` and fails until they are. `fences.js` needs adding to `BASE` in
`tests/layers.test.js:143`, which is what makes "it may not import `state.js`"
executable rather than advisory.

No new CSS file: fence rules go in `items.css`, which is where cards-per-type
live. That keeps `APP_CSS_ORDER` and the stylesheet order alone.

### Changed, because a fence is a card that travels

| file | change |
| --- | --- |
| `state.js` | re-export the `fences.js` surface under the façade, as it does for `sticky.js` |
| `canvas/input.js` | union `fenceFollowers` into the three follower sites |
| `layout.js` | `refence(driven)` beside `restick(driven)` in `commitGeom`; park fences off the Mobile board in `completeLayout()`; filter them out of `placeMobileItems()`'s obstacles |
| `stacking.js` | `stackRoot()` walks fence membership |
| `canvas/renderers.js` | one entry in `RENDERERS` |
| `commands.js` | `addFence`, and `fenceSelection` — draw a fence around what is selected, which is the gesture that will actually get used |
| `index.html` | one `<button data-cmd>` in `#toolbar-tools`, if it earns a toolbar slot |
| `items.css` | the card |

### Changed, because a fence is not content

This is the list that gets forgotten, and each one is a small silent bug if it is
missed:

| site | why |
| --- | --- |
| `hasContent()` in `state.js` | a board of empty fences is an empty board — the ghost cards should still be showing |
| `ensureGhostCards()` / `dismissGhosts()` | ditto: drawing a fence must not dismiss the hints |
| `paletteSources` / `ui/pigments.js` | a fence has no picture; it must not count toward the Dynamic palette's sources |
| the HUD count in `ui/hud.js` | "42 items" should not include the boxes drawn around them |
| `ui/search.js` | a fence's name *should* be findable — this one is the exception, and jumping to a fence is arguably the feature |
| `optimize/` | `WORTH_IT` must not see a fence as something to compress |
| `sticky.js` `measureStick` | a note must not stick to a fence, for the same reason it may not stick to the title card |
| `web-graph.js` `connectSelection` | a spanning tree over fences draws lines between regions; probably wrong, certainly untested |

`isFurniture()` is **not** the right switch for most of these. Its doc comment is
specific: it means "the app put this here, not the user", and a fence is the
user's. It is worth reading that comment before reaching for it — it exists
precisely because this question was spelled out at four sites and drifted.

### The format

`docs/mbrd-format.md` gains:

- `fence` in the `type` list.
- `meta.fence` in the meta table, on any type: the id of the fence a card is
  inside, or `null`. Stamped at save, seeds the runtime memo, dangling ids fall
  back to measuring.
- `meta.label` or reuse of `name` on a fence — `name` is right; a fence's label
  is a card's name and `editItemName()` already edits it.
- If roll-up ships: `meta.rolled`, a boolean.

Every one of those is an additive `meta` key or a new `type` value, which the
document's own compatibility rules call free. **No version bump.** Say so
explicitly in the changelog, because "a new item type" sounds like one and is
not.

## Staging

**v1 — the fence exists.** Draw one, name it, drag it and its cards come; resize
it and membership follows the edge; it saves, loads, bins, restores and undoes.
Desktop only. `cmds.addFence` and `cmds.fenceSelection`.

**v2 — the fence is useful.** Roll-up (double-click the bar and the fence
collapses to its label, hiding its members — Fences' signature gesture and the
thing that actually makes a crowded desktop legible). Per-fence tint. Arrange
*within* a fence, which is `arrange/arrangements.js` called with a bounding box
it already accepts.

**v3 — Mobile**, as answer (b) above, in its own document.

Roll-up is the one v2 item worth pricing now, because it is the only feature here
that makes a fence hide something. Hidden items interact with culling, with
search (a hit inside a rolled fence has to open it), with the HUD count, and with
export. None of that is hard; all of it is a decision, and it is cheaper to know
that before v1's data model is written than after.

## What could go wrong

- **A fence spanning many spatial cells.** `canvas/spatial.js` buckets at 512
  world units, so a 4000×3000 fence is registered in ~48 cells and every rebuild
  walks them. Not a blocker — the module's header already anticipates it and says
  `rebuild()` can override the cell size per board — but a board of large fences
  is the case that would find out, and it should be measured rather than assumed.
- **A fence bigger than the viewport, culled by its centre.** Culling tests the
  true rectangle after the grid narrows the field, so this *should* be fine. It
  is worth an explicit test, because a fence you can only see by zooming out is a
  bad first impression and the failure is invisible on a small board.
- **The membership memo and undo.** Undoing a move restores geometry; it must
  also restore what was in what. The memo is keyed by member id, and `restick()`
  is called from `commitGeom` — which undo does not go through. Worth checking
  whether sticky notes already have this bug before deciding it is a fence
  problem; if they do, it is one fix for both and belongs in `sticky.js`.
- **Two mechanisms that are 80% the same.** `sticky.js` and `fences.js` will read
  alike, and the temptation will be to abstract them into one "what follows what"
  module. Resist it until there is a third: the predicates differ (overlap vs
  containment, notes-only vs any type, re-measure on resize or not), and an
  abstraction over two cases would hide exactly the differences that are the
  design. `stackRoot()` is the one place they should meet.

## Tests

Unit, in `tests/`:

- centre-in-rect membership, including a card straddling an edge, and the
  smallest-fence-wins rule for nesting;
- moving a fence carries its members and does **not** swallow what it passes
  over;
- resizing a fence **does** re-measure — both halves, because each is surprising
  alone;
- a card dragged out of a fence is loose after the drop and not before;
- `fenceFollowers` ∪ `stuckFollowers` reaches a fixed point: a note stuck to a
  card in a fence travels with the fence, and is moved exactly once;
- `meta.fence` round-trips through `serializeBoard`/`loadBoard`, and a dangling
  id falls through to measuring;
- a fence does not count toward `hasContent()`, and drawing one does not dismiss
  the ghosts.

End-to-end, in `tests/e2e/board.spec.js`: draw a fence around two cards, drag it,
assert both moved; refresh and assert the grouping survived.
