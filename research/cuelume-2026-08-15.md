# Interface sounds — 2026-08-15

*The design for **L5** in `open-work-2026-08-14.md`. This document has an end: it
goes to `old/` on the commit that ships the feature, in the shape
`plan-2026-08-14.md` kept. Until then it is the argument, and the register is the
row that says the work exists.*

The app makes no sound of its own today. `canvas/audio.ts` plays the board's
content — a clip on a card, a queue in the Playlist lens — and that is a
different thing entirely: it is somebody else's recording, played because it was
asked for. This is the interface saying something back.

The starting point is **Cuelume** (MIT, `Danilaa1/cuelume`, v0.2.2): four
TypeScript files, no dependencies, seventeen sounds synthesized live on one
`AudioContext` with no audio files anywhere. It was read in full on 2026-08-15
and about two thirds of it is being taken.

---

## What is taken, and what is thrown away

Cuelume is two halves that happen to ship together.

The **engine** is the half worth having. `recipes.ts` is a table — each sound is
a handful of numbers: waveform, frequency, an optional glide, attack, decay, and
sometimes a filtered noise layer or a feedback delay for a tail. `engine.ts`
renders one of those into Web Audio nodes on a shared, lazily created context,
through one boosted output stage with a compressor behind it so overlapping cues
do not clip. It is careful in the ways this codebase is careful: it bails when
Web Audio is missing, it bails before the first user gesture, it disconnects its
nodes on a timer sized to the sound's own tail, and it never touches `document`.

The **binder** is the half being thrown away. `bind()` installs four
capture-phase listeners on `document` — `pointerenter`, `pointerdown`,
`pointerup`, `click` — and reads `data-cuelume-*` attributes off whatever the
event landed on. For a library that is exactly right: it cannot know what the
host app's actions are, so it lets the markup say.

It is wrong here for three reasons that stack.

**It is a second event listener, which `CLAUDE.md` forbids in as many words.**
A user-facing action is an entry in `cmds`. Four global listeners in the capture
phase is the opposite of that, and they land in the busiest part of the app: the
menu closes on outside `pointerdown` in the capture phase, and `canvas/input.ts`
is one pipeline holding exactly one active gesture. Nothing here calls
`preventDefault` or `stopPropagation`, so this is not a bug waiting to happen so
much as a second input surface nobody asked for, ordered by registration.

**It is a second markup contract.** `data-cmd` already says what a button does.
`data-cuelume-press` beside it would say what it sounds like, in a different
vocabulary, maintained separately, and drifting the first time a button is
renamed.

**Most of what it can reach is not what should make a sound.** The events below
are card landings, connections, deletions, undo — none of them are clicks on an
element with an attribute. And the one thing `bind()` is genuinely built for,
hover ticks, is not wanted: on a canvas you sweep the pointer across dozens of
cards without meaning anything by it, and a sound there is noise in the literal
sense.

So: `recipes.ts` and `engine.ts` come across as source. `bind.ts` and
`index.ts` do not.

## Where it sits in the graph

`cuelume/recipes.ts` and `cuelume/engine.ts` go in `BASE` in
`tests/layers.test.js`, and the argument is the one already made there four
times. They import nothing but each other and `prefs.ts`. They are read from
`state.ts`, `canvas/`, `ui/` and `notify.ts` — four tiers — and a thing every
tier reads has to be below all of them or it is an inversion for most of them.
It is the same sentence that put `color.ts`, `notify.ts`, `quality.ts` and
`media/transport.ts` down there.

The comparison worth making is `quality.ts`, because the shape is identical: a
small dial, stored per person in `localStorage`, read by modules that must not
import the interface, written by one control in the panel, and deliberately not
an event on `state.ts`'s bus because it is not board state.

The bench is `ui/sound-lab.ts`, beside `ui/pigments.ts`, for the same reason that
one is in `ui/`: it is a page, and a page is interface.

The name stays **cuelume** everywhere — directory, module, licence file. It is a
fork of something with a name, and renaming it would make the provenance harder
to see for no gain.

## The cues, and the whimsy axis

**Nothing at a call site names a sound.** Call sites name a *cue*, the cue and
the current whimsy tier pick the recipe, and the map from one to the other lives
in `recipes.ts`. This is the same move `canvas/exit-anim.ts` already makes with
`TIER_KIND` — the tier picks the feel, the caller says only what happened — and
it is what stops seventeen recipe names leaking into fifteen modules.

Eight cues:

| cue | fires on |
| --- | --- |
| `tap` | a card lands after a drag; a sticker is placed |
| `on` / `off` | a toggle — grid, axes, readout; a fence closing |
| `rise` / `fall` | redo / undo; restore / delete; a connection made / removed |
| `done` | a board saved; an import finished |
| `fail` | an error |
| `note` | every other toast |
| `arrive` | a board opened |
| `sweep` | a marquee closing on a non-empty selection |

Two of those are pairs rather than two entries that happen to be near each other.
`rise`/`fall` and `on`/`off` are **the same recipe with the glide inverted**, so
redo is audibly undo backwards and closing is audibly the opposite of opening.
That is a thing you can only do when the sounds are numbers rather than files,
and it is the single strongest argument for having taken a synth instead of a
sprite of `.wav`s.

**The whimsy axis carries sound, and it carries it as character rather than as
volume.** The axis already moves shape, type, motion, elevation, ornament and
contrast together; the interface at Softish is not a quieter version of the
interface at Harsh, it is a warmer one, and the sound has to work the same way or
it will be the one thing on the dial that reads as a loudness control. Same
number of cues at every stop. Different family:

- **Softish** leans on the bell end — `chime`, `bloom`, `droplet`, `whisper`,
  `sparkle`.
- **Middle** on the mechanical — `press`, `release`, `toggle`, `success`, `page`.
- **Harsh** on the synthetic — `tick`, `pulse`, `scan`, `ready`, `loading`.

Eight cues across three tiers is what gives all seventeen recipes a job. Without
the axis, roughly half of them ship unused, which under this codebase's
one-entry-per-thing habit is dead weight.

The exact assignment is **not settled here**, and settling it by reading a table
would be the same mistake `old/feedback-plan-2026-08-14.md` records four times:
four of its twelve diagnoses were wrong, and every one of them was found by
opening the app rather than by reading the source. The equivalent here is
listening. That is what the bench is for, and it is why the bench is in the same
commit rather than after it.

## Repeats

Drag forty cards and forty of them land. The instinct is to throttle, and the
decision is not to: forty taps should sound like forty taps, because the
alternative is an interface that goes quiet exactly when the most is happening.
Undo held down should stutter for the same reason — the stutter *is* the feedback
that many steps are going by.

Two guards, both small, so that stutter does not become a buzz:

- **A same-cue repeat inside about 30ms is dropped.** Two identical transients
  that close together are not heard as two events anyway; they are heard as one
  louder one, which is the wrong feedback.
- **Consecutive repeats detune by a few cents.** Identical waveforms starting in
  lockstep phase-lock into a tone. A little variation keeps forty taps sounding
  like forty separate small things.

The shared limiter Cuelume already puts after its output stage handles the
overlap itself. Nothing else is needed.

## Sound the app makes while sound the app plays

The one real hazard is a phone. A live `AudioContext` can interrupt or duck audio
playing in another app, and this app is a moodboard — somebody arranging pictures
with music on in the background is the ordinary case, not the edge one.

**There is no way to duck from a web page.** No API offers it. What there is is
`navigator.audioSession.type`, and setting it to `'ambient'` says: mix with
whatever else is playing, interrupt nothing, and be silenced by the ringer
switch. That is the right category for a blip that is a hundred milliseconds
long, and it is the same category a keyboard click sound uses.

Feature-detected, because it is a Safari API today — which is convenient, since
iOS is where the interruption risk mostly lives. Elsewhere a short Web Audio
sound generally mixes anyway. **This is a claim to verify on a real phone rather
than one to take from this document**, and it belongs in
`docs/browser-support.md` once it is verified.

The second half of the same problem is that the app would now hold a live audio
context where it has never held one before. Cuelume never closes its context.
This fork **suspends after about five seconds of silence and resumes on the next
cue**, which costs a few milliseconds on the first sound after a quiet spell and
removes the standing cost the rest of the time.

`canvas/audio.ts`'s media path is untouched and unrelated: it plays through
`<audio>` elements, and `canvas/waveform.ts` and `optimize/opus.ts` use
`OfflineAudioContext`, which renders nothing to a speaker. Three audio subsystems
that do not meet.

## The setting

One control, in the **Quality** block on the System tab, because it is the same
kind of thing everything else in that block is: a fact about this machine and the
person at it, not about the board. It never travels inside a `.mbrd`.

```
{ id: 'opt-sound', type: 'range', label: 'Sounds', silent: true,
  min: 0, max: 3, step: 1, stops: ['Off', 'Low', 'Medium', 'High'] }
```

A four-stop slider with named stops, the shape Whimsy and Quality already use —
legible in a way tick marks are not, and one control instead of a checkbox plus a
volume that can disagree with it.

**Default Medium.** On by default is the decision that makes this worth building;
a feature nobody finds is a feature nobody has. Off is one drag away and it
sticks.

Stored as `mbrd.sound` through `prefs.ts`, which means two things are already
handled and need no code: `clearPrefs()` sweeps it by prefix, and `freezePrefs()`
stops `/patch` recording anything a reader does to it.

Silent regardless of the slider when:

- the level is Off;
- the operating system asks for reduced motion — there is no equivalent
  preference for sound, and somebody who has turned animation down has said
  something close enough to be worth honouring;
- the page is `/patch`, which is a document rather than a workspace and does not
  get to make noise at somebody reading release notes.

The quality dial does **not** touch it. Sound costs nothing to draw, and muting
it on a slow phone would be tidying rather than helping.

## What changes in the engine

1. `bind.ts` and `index.ts` are not ported.
2. `navigator.audioSession.type = 'ambient'` where the API exists.
3. Suspend after silence; resume on the next cue.
4. `setEnabled`/`setVolume` are replaced by one level read from `prefs.ts`,
   plus the three mute rules above.
5. The cue → tier → recipe map is added to `recipes.ts`.
6. The repeat guard and the detune.
7. Mechanical: import specifiers carry the real `.ts` extension, single quotes.
8. **Both headers rewritten.** Every file in this codebase argues its own design
   at the top; two that do not would read as foreign, and the thing most worth
   writing down is what was changed from upstream and why. The MIT copyright line
   stays in both.

Upstream is already clean under `erasableSyntaxOnly` and `verbatimModuleSyntax` —
string-literal unions, no enums, no parameter properties, `import { type X }`
where it means a type. It touches `window` only inside functions, so
`tests/imports.test.js` needs no new exemption.

## The bench

`web/lab-sound.html`, built by `npm run dev:sound` into
`web/assets/lab-sound.js`, added to `npm run build` beside the pigment lab.

Every cue as a button, a tier switch, and sliders over the recipe numbers, so a
change is audible immediately rather than after a rebuild. The whole point is
that picking sounds by reading a table of adjectives does not work.

Same rules `lab.html` states for itself, and for the same reasons: its own
bundle, `noindex` rather than a `robots.txt` disallow, absent from `sitemap.xml`,
absent from `SHELL`. It is a bench, not a page of the site.

## Tests

`tests/cuelume.test.js`:

- **Upstream's `runtime.test.mjs`, adapted.** It is 439 lines and it fakes the
  whole Web Audio surface as globals, which is worth far more than writing one
  from scratch. Retargeted from `../dist/` to the `.ts` sources, which Node runs
  directly. One thing to check when doing it: it cache-busts with
  `import('…?activation=' + Date.now())`, and that query on a `.ts` specifier may
  or may not survive Node's stripper. If it does not, the stub is reset another
  way.
- **Every cue resolves to a real recipe at all three tiers.** This is the
  `icons.test.js` case exactly: a misspelled recipe name fails *silently*, on the
  ear, in one tier, and nothing else will ever catch it.
- **The default level is Medium**, and the stored value round-trips and clamps.
- **Reduced motion mutes.**
- **The 30ms repeat guard drops the second of a pair and not the first.**
- `BASE` gains two entries in `tests/layers.test.js`.

`tests/csp.test.js` is untouched, and that is worth stating rather than assuming:
no network, no inline script, no new embed host, no new `connect-src`. This
feature is invisible to the policy.

## What must be called out

No `.mbrd` schema change. No generated-catalogue change.

**`SHELL` in `web/sw.js` gains one entry**, `./assets/cuelume-LICENSE.txt`,
following the precedent the sticker licence set: an installed app that carries
somebody else's work offline carries the notice with it. No apostrophes anywhere
near it — `tests/sw.test.js` reads that list by pulling out single-quoted runs.

**Licensing.** MIT into GPL-3.0-or-later is fine in that direction, but the MIT
notice condition is a condition: the copyright line stays in both vendored
headers, the licence file ships beside them, and `THIRD-PARTY.md` gains a row.
Its opening sentence — that the list is short because it is the bundled faces and
one sprite of drawings — stops being true and has to be edited rather than
appended to.

`patch-notes.md` is written by hand and is not this document's business, beyond
the standing rule that the newest `version:` span's high end must match `VERSION`
when this lands.

## Open, and deliberately

**The recipe assignment.** Eight cues, three tiers, seventeen recipes, and the
table above is a first guess made by reading adjectives. The bench exists because
that is not how it gets decided. Expect the map to change before the commit
lands, and expect some recipes to be re-tuned rather than merely reassigned —
a `press` a little deeper, a `success` a little shorter, a tail removed.

**Whether `note` survives contact.** Every toast making a sound includes the
quiet ones — *Board saved*, *3 cards added*. That may be right and may be too
much; `note` is the first cue to cut if it turns out to be chatter, and cutting
it is one line.

**The marquee.** Taken as: one cue when the marquee closes having selected
something, nothing while dragging, nothing on an empty selection. Written down
because it was an assumption rather than a decision.

**iOS.** Whether `'ambient'` genuinely leaves background music alone is the one
claim in here that no test can make. It wants a phone.
