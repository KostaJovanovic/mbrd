## The finding, in one paragraph

The app is in better shape than a pass like this usually finds, and the
interesting result is not the bug list. It is that **more than half of what a
driven browser reports as broken in this app is the driving, not the app** — the
idle fade eats the first click, `requestAnimationFrame` stops entirely when the
window is occluded, a harness that sends `shiftKey: false` makes marquee select
look dead, and a card measured in one round trip has moved by the next. Every one
of those produced a confident false positive that survived until it was checked a
second way. What is actually wrong is four things, and only the first is visible
without looking for it: a hover flyout is clamped to the window when it should be
clamped to the bar it hangs from, and Arrange is far enough right that it hangs
off the end.

## Finding 1 — A flyout is clamped to the window, not to the bar

Hover Arrange and the panel comes down 28px past the right-hand end of the
toolbar. Measured, with the flyout up:

| | left | right |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `#toolbar` | 367 | **1061** |
| `#ctx-menu.is-flyout` | 881 | **1089** |

This is worse than a menu that is merely off-centre, and the reason is in
`menu.css:171`. `#ctx-menu.is-flyout` deliberately drops its top border and both
top corner radii so the panel reads as *the underside of the bar* — the comment
there says so outright, and calls a rounded lip with its own hairline a pixel
below the bar's "a second object that has landed on the first". That illusion is
load-bearing, and it only holds while the panel is over the bar. Past the end,
the squared top-right corner is a cut edge hanging against the board, which is
precisely the shape of a rendering mistake.

The cause is one expression. `render()` in `ui/menu.ts:682`:

```js
x = Math.min(Math.max(pad, clientX), Math.max(pad, innerWidth - width - pad));
```

The only horizontal bound is the window, and the window is nowhere near. The
information needed is already in hand one frame earlier: `flyout.ts:217` builds
the anchor rect and already calls `bar.getBoundingClientRect()` to get `bottom`.
It takes the bar's bottom edge and throws away its left and right.

Note and Colour sit mid-bar and measured well inside it — overhangs of −406 and
−324 — so Arrange is the only button showing this today. It is still the general
rule that is wrong, and a fourth flyout added anywhere right of centre would show
it too, so the bound belongs in `render()` rather than in a special case.

**What to do.** Pass the bar's rect from `flyout.ts` as a `bounds` option through
`openAnchored()` (`menu.ts:138`) into the `opts.anchor` branch, and clamp inside
it intersected with the window, so a bar wider than the viewport cannot push the
panel off-screen:

```js
const lo = Math.max(pad, Math.min(opts.bounds?.left ?? pad, innerWidth - width - pad));
const hi = Math.min(opts.bounds ? opts.bounds.right - width : Infinity,
                    innerWidth - width - pad);
x = Math.min(Math.max(lo, clientX), Math.max(lo, hi));
```

The `Math.max(lo, hi)` is not decoration: a container narrower than the panel
would otherwise inverting-clamp, and this degrades it to "align to the
container's left edge" instead.

**Clamp, not flip**, and the existing comment at `menu.ts:678-681` is the reason
— a flyout that jumped to the far side of its button would leave the pointer
outside itself and close on the way in. Clamping to the bar keeps that argument
whole: 208px of panel against 694px of bar still lands under Arrange.

The phone's More button (`commands.ts:1006`) is the other `openAnchored()` caller,
passes no bounds, and is untouched.

## Finding 2 — Join writes lines that nothing can draw

Arm Join, tap a sticker, tap the title card. The pair is written to
`board.connections`, saved, and **survives a reload** — and is never drawn. No
toast, no refusal, no mark on the sticker. The only way to discover it is to read
the board file.

Three correct decisions meet and leave a hole between them:

- `connectStep()` (`ui/toolbar.ts:141`) is four lines and takes any two distinct
  ids. It is pure and testable and asks no questions about what an id names.
- `centres()` (`canvas/web.ts:477`) filters the ends it will place through
  `isJoinEnd`, so a pair naming anything else is skipped in `build()`.
- `isJoinEnd` (`board-model.ts:518`) excludes `ghost` and `sticker`, and its
  comment argues the case well: a sticker is *a remark about* the card underneath
  it, so a line from a star to a photograph is a line from a thing to what it
  already says.

None of the three is wrong. What is wrong is that the tap path never asks the
question the draw path answers. The evidence that the layer itself is healthy is
that a note↔title pair drawn the same way renders immediately.

The selection path already gets this right, which is what makes it a defect
rather than a design gap: `cmds.connect` filters its seed through `joinable()`
(`commands.ts:155`), and `joinable` applies `isJoinEnd`. Two doors into one
feature, disagreeing about what may go through.

**What to do.** Put the check in `connectStep()`, where the rest of the step
logic lives and where a unit test can reach it. A tap on a non-join-end must not
consume the standing pick — an accidental tap on a sticker should not cost the
pair being built — and `connectTap()` should still return `true` so the press does
not fall through into selection. One toast, in the voice of the connect toasts
already there.

**This is a `.mbrd` data question as well as a UI one.** Boards saved before the
fix can already carry pairs naming a sticker, and they are unreachable state that
can never become valid. Dropping them on load is the honest cleanup; leaving them
means every such board carries invisible cargo forever.

## Finding 3 — The sticker pad opens ten pixels down

`.sticker-window-body` opens at `scrollTop: 10.4` where it should be `0`. With
`padding-top: 4px` above it, the first category heading — *Marks* — is sliced
along the top of its letters. Small, and the kind of thing that reads as a font
problem rather than a scroll problem, which is why it is worth naming.

Near-certainly a focus-driven `scrollIntoView` on the first tile as the window
builds. The fix is either a `scrollTop = 0` after the build in
`ui/sticker-window.ts`, or focusing the tile with `focus({ preventScroll: true })`
— the same guard `menu.ts:710` already uses for the same reason.

**Corrected 2026-08-14, without a browser: it is neither.** `ui/sticker-window.ts`
calls `.focus()` nowhere at all, so there is no `scrollIntoView` to guard. The
last line of the build is the whole of it:

```js
const at = body.querySelector(`[data-cat="${lastCategory}"]`);
if (at) body.scrollTop = at.offsetTop - body.offsetTop;
```

That is the *remember which shelf you were on* feature, and it fires on the
first category as readily as on the ninth — `lastCategory` initialises to
`STICKER_CATEGORIES[0][0]`, which is `marks`, so on a fresh window the target
*is* Marks and the line still runs. `offsetTop` is measured from the offset
parent (`.sticker-window`, the only positioned ancestor), so for the first
heading the difference is not zero: it is the body's `padding-top` plus the
heading's `margin-top`.

**Where this stops being verified.** Those two are `4px` and `12px`, which is
16 and not 10.4, so the static values do not reproduce the measurement and one
of the two is wrong. Either the number came off the same harness this document
spends a section distrusting, or something else moves the body between build and
measure. The *mechanism* is solid — nothing else in the module writes
`scrollTop`, there is no `focus()` and no `scrollIntoView` — but the arithmetic
wants a real browser before anyone quotes it.

Either way the fix is a guard rather than a reset: leave `scrollTop` alone when
the target is the first child, or measure the two against a common origin
(`at.offsetTop - body.firstElementChild.offsetTop`). A blanket `scrollTop = 0`
would fix the symptom by deleting the feature — and would be a no-op the moment
somebody has a favourite, because the Favourites block then sits above Marks and
a real scroll is correct.

## Finding 4 — Two rows claim the same gesture

On an image's context menu, **Open** and **Zoom to it** both print `dbl-click` in
the accelerator column. Only one of them can be true: `input.ts:1872` sends every
item that is not a note and not the title card to `openViewer`, so on a
photograph the gesture opens the viewer and the Zoom row's accel
(`menu.ts:387`) is stale.

A menu that advertises the same key twice teaches the wrong thing about both
rows. Drop the accel from Zoom when the item is viewable, or drop it outright and
let Open own the gesture.

## Unfinished — double-click to open

One attempt landed provably on an image card — `elementFromPoint` returned the
card, the item became selected, and a real `dblclick` fired — and the viewer did
not open. Instrumenting the event showed its `target` was `#viewport`, not the
card, so `itemIdFromEvent(e.target)` returns null and the handler takes its
"not on a card" branch and looks for a connection instead. A `dblclick` targets
the nearest common ancestor of its two clicks, so this happens whenever the two
clicks resolve to different elements.

It could not be reproduced reliably: the window was being resized between round
trips, which re-lays-out the board and invalidates every coordinate measured in
the call before. `cmds.openViewer(id)` works when called directly and the viewer
itself is sound, so if this is real it is a targeting fault and nothing deeper.

**Check it by hand on a still window before believing it.** If it is real, the
answer is to stop trusting the DOM `dblclick` target: resolve the id by
hit-testing `vp.toWorld(e.clientX, e.clientY)`, or track the second press in the
pointer pipeline that already owns `DOUBLE_TAP_MS` (`input.ts:89`) and already
knows which card it is on.

## What the harness got wrong, and why it is worth writing down

Every item here was reported by the driven pass as a defect and is not one. They
are recorded because the next person to drive this app will hit the same four,
and because three of them are properties of *this* app's design rather than of
browser automation in general.

- **The chrome swallows the first click.** After fifteen seconds `is-idle` takes
  `pointer-events: none` off the toolbar, bin and corner (`chrome.css:585-615`),
  so a click lands on the board and only wakes the furniture. Undo and Redo both
  looked dead this way and both work with the bar awake. This is deliberate and
  the comment argues it for touch — the first tap must bring the bar back rather
  than land on an invisible Empty. A driver must move the pointer first; a hand
  always has.
- **`requestAnimationFrame` stops dead when the window is occluded.** With
  `visibilityState: "hidden"` no frame ever runs, and `requestBuild()` defers to
  a rAF throttle — so connections looked as though they were stored and never
  drawn, for minutes, until a real drag woke the compositor and every one of them
  appeared at once. This produced the single most convincing false finding of the
  pass. Anything drawn through `requestBuild()`/`requestPaint()` must be verified
  with the window in the foreground.
- **Modifier keys did not survive the drag.** The pointerdown arrived with
  `shiftKey: false`, so shift-drag panned instead of banding and marquee select
  looked missing. Dispatching the same gesture with the modifier actually set
  selects correctly, exactly as `input.ts:28` documents.
- **Coordinates go stale between round trips.** The board pans, the player opens
  and shifts the layout, the window resizes. A rect measured in one call is
  fiction by the next, and a click on it lands on bare board — which reads as
  "the card ignores clicks".

Two more, particular to the app and both correct behaviour:

- The **Feed's image tile renders blank** for a moment: the tile's `<img>` is
  `loading="lazy"` and simply had not painted. It loads and draws.
- The **viewer leaves its `<img>` mounted** after close, which looks like the
  teardown leaking. It is deliberate — `teardown()` releases only `<video>` and
  `<audio>`, because every URL in there comes from `assetURL()`, is minted per
  asset for the life of the board, and is the same URL the card behind the dialog
  is still showing. Revoking it would blank the card.

And two that were read as faults and are decisions: **Reload board** appearing on
an item's menu (`menu.ts:370-374` argues it — a right-click is the way back to
the board's own actions from wherever the pointer is), and the note composer's
`A− A+` row wrapping to a second line, which is correctly centred flex-wrap.

## What was exercised

Toolbar flyouts on all three buttons; the note composer with its formatting bar;
item and canvas context menus; zoom in, out and fit — fit verified properly, with
every item inside the viewport afterwards; the scale bar; all three sidebar tabs
and the palette and grid toggles; stickers, arm through place; card drag; undo
and redo by button and by `Ctrl+Z`; Find; save, reload and restore; the viewer's
head, metadata, scroller and teardown; audio playback with the now-playing bar,
its scrubber, volume and queue; **Feed**; **Playlist**, with cover art, "9 songs,
34 min", Play and Shuffle and nine rows; the trash panel with its entry, age,
Empty and restore hint; the Link dialog; marquee select. The boot console was
clean in every mode and no errors were logged at any point.

Not reached: Files and drag-and-drop import, which need a real file picker;
export, share, PDF and image export, which write downloads; fences beyond the
marquee that feeds them; and the phone *viewport* tier — the Feed profile was
exercised, but at desktop width, so the phone toolbar and the `#toolbar` before
`#nowplaying` sibling-combinator rule were never on screen.
