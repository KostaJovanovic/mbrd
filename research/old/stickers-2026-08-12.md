# Stickers, and stickiness that actually sticks

*Written 2026-08-12, before any of it existed, and carried out on the same day.
History, not a specification — where this disagrees with the code, the code is
right. The status block below is the half worth reading if something looks
wrong.*

## Status: all six stages landed, with six departures

Built in the order staged below: `meta.loose` and pin enforcement, the
`isSticky()` sweep, the `sticker` type and its sprite, the pad, Mobile
placement, and the trimmings. `npm test` runs 1037 and passes 1036, including a
new `tests/stickers.test.js` — the sprite against the catalogue in both
directions, the travel relation end to end, the delete cascade, the loose flag
through a save and an undo, and the settle window against a wound-forward clock
— plus `tests/stickers-license.test.js` and `tests/credits.test.js`. The one
failure is `icons.test.js`'s orphan check on `i-more`, which belongs to the
in-flight `ui/conn-chip.js` work: that module reaches the sprite through
`button('i-more')` rather than a `<use href>`, and the test only sweeps for the
latter. It fails on this branch with or without any of this change.

Six places the code does not do what this document says, each because building
it made the reason plain. The last three are the interesting ones — they are
changes of mind after seeing the thing on a board, not corrections to the plan
as reasoning:

1. **`wouldStick()` is not guarded by `meta.loose`.** The plan says the guard
   goes in both it and `stuckTo()`. It cannot: `wouldStick()` is the question a
   drag in flight asks, and a drag that finds a host is precisely the way back
   from Unstick — guarding it would take that door away. It also takes a bare
   box rather than an item, so there is no flag there to read. The guard is in
   `stuckTo()` alone, which is one line and the whole implementation.

2. **`resettle()` runs from `canvas/input.js`, not from `commitGeom()`.** The
   obvious home was beside `restick()` inside the committed pair, and it is the
   wrong one twice over. Inside, undo would re-derive the flag instead of
   restoring it, so "loose" could never survive a step back; and it would fire
   on the arrow keys, which *set* the flag on an item still lying on its old
   host and would have had it taken straight off again. It runs once, in front
   of the commit, and `snapshotGeom()` carries the flag in both directions from
   there. The asymmetry the plan asked for falls out of that placement.

3. **`isContent()` was not doing two jobs.** *Still open* item 5 worried that
   connection eligibility and fence membership shared a predicate and that
   stickers wanted opposite answers. They did not share one: connections went
   through `isRider()` and a bare `type !== 'ghost'`, and fence membership
   through `fences.js`. The exclusion is a new `isJoinEnd()` in
   `board-model.js`, and its doc comment records the trap the plan spotted —
   because the tempting shortcut *was* to reach for `isContent()`, and that
   would have taken loose stickers out of fences to keep them out of the web.

4. **The artwork is not die-cut vinyl, and it is not ours.** The plan settled on
   filled silhouettes with a white keyline, in a fixed palette of named
   primaries, and argued for both. Both were overruled after seeing them on a
   board: a stock red heart in the middle of a papyrus-and-terracotta sheet is
   the "stray office-yellow rectangle" the head of `tokens.css` warns about, one
   relation over. What shipped is a **paper body with an inked outline** — the
   reading the app's own icons have — in eight inks mixed out of the palette's
   own pigment, so what "red" means is whatever this board's red is.

   The shapes were then hand-drawn in that language and replaced again by
   **Phosphor** (MIT), vendored at a pinned revision by `tools/gen-stickers.mjs`.
   Its *duotone* weight is that construction already drawn — a background
   silhouette and the outline over it — and nine thousand icons drawn to one
   grid by people doing nothing else are more consistent with each other than
   forty-five drawn here could be. Two knock-on effects worth knowing: nothing
   is stroked any more (Phosphor draws every weight as a filled path, so there
   is no line-weight token), and the marks that *are* a line take the *bold*
   weight instead, because Phosphor gives a line glyph a generic rounded-square
   plate as its duotone background and a plus sticker on a paper card is a
   different object.

   Five ids moved in the process and one shape changed identity:
   `s-star-outline` became `s-star-four`, `s-burst` `s-sparkle`,
   `s-heart-broken` `s-heart-break`, `s-exclamation-double` `s-warning`,
   `s-interrobang` `s-question-circle`, and the OK hand became an eye — it
   cannot be drawn in this language without its fingers crossing the ring, and
   an eye is more use on a moodboard anyway. Free, since no board in the wild
   had a sticker on it yet.

5. **A stuck item does not pin instantly — it sets after ten seconds.** The plan
   has pinning arrive with the drop, and it is a trap: you put a sticky on a
   photograph, see it is two millimetres off, reach for it, and the photograph
   moves. Right-click, Unstick, nudge, and a three-second job has cost four
   steps. So sticking stays instant (it has to — a photograph dragged straight
   after must take the note along) and *pinning* waits out `SETTLE_MS`.
   `isPinned()` becomes `stuckTo() && !isSettling()`; nothing else in the plan
   changes, because everything else was already reading that one predicate.

   It needs no timer and stores nothing: the two things that read it ask at the
   moment they run, and a board being opened is a board that has been sitting
   still. `Unstick` widened to `isRider()` to match, so you can refuse the pin
   during the window rather than waiting for it in order to undo it.

6. **The sprite went down once, silently, and it is worth recording how.** SVG
   is XML, and two hyphens in a row inside a comment is a parse error — which
   takes down the *whole file*, so every shape on every board drew nothing at
   once, with no console warning and no failed request to say why. The cause was
   the section headings, written as rules of hyphens in the house style the rest
   of this codebase uses. Both sprites now have a test for it.

Two things the plan named that were done differently in kind rather than in
substance: the move and resize gestures were lifted out of `ui/playlist.js` into
a shared `ui/float-window.js` instead of being copied, and `showStickTarget()`
moved out of `canvas/input.js`'s closure into `canvas/items.js`, which owns the
nodes it marks — the pad needed the same ring and could not reach into a
function.

*Still open* items 1 (loose stickers in the Mobile feed), 2 (the pin badge), 3
(re-rolling tilt on a hand-rotated sticker) and 4 (the tint set) are all still
open, and all four now have something on screen to judge them against. Item 6
answered itself: favourites went into `localStorage` through `readPrefJSON` /
`writePref` in `util.js`, which is where the volume and the panel state already
live — neither `storage/session.js` nor `storage/library.js` was the door.

## The two asks

**1. Stuck means fixed.** Today a note stuck to a card *travels* with the card
but is still free to be picked up and dragged off it at any moment. The ask is
that once a note is on a card it is **pinned there** — a drag on it does not move
it, and the only way out is right-click → **Unstick**, after which it moves
freely again.

**2. Stickers.** A new kind of thing — stars, hearts, exclamation marks, question
marks, arrows, checks, and a good many more — that sticks to a card exactly the
way a note does, and is dragged onto the board out of **its own little floating
window**, the way the Playlist window sits on the Desktop board.

The second is mostly new surface. The first is a change to the semantics of a
relation that four other modules already depend on, and it is the one that can
break boards people already have. It goes first, and it goes in on its own.

## Where stickiness lives today

`web/assets/js/sticky.js` (300 lines, one of the base layers under `state.js`,
listed in `tests/layers.test.js` and forbidden from importing `state.js` back).
Read its header before touching anything here; the argument in it is the thing
this plan is proposing to bend.

The current model, in four facts:

- **Stuckness is measured, not stored.** `measureStick()` asks which item more
  than `STICK_MIN` (5%) of the note's area lies over, topmost-below-it wins. The
  file's header argues at length that a *stored* relation could disagree with its
  own geometry, which would be a hazard with no upside.
- **It is memoised until the note itself is handled.** `sticks: Map<noteId,
  hostId|null>`; `restick(ids)` is called with the ids a gesture *drove*, never
  with the followers it towed. So moving the photo does not re-parent the notes
  on it; picking the note up does.
- **`meta.stuckTo` is stamped at serialize time** (`state.js:2304`) and reseeded
  on load by `seedSticks()`, purely to survive a pixel of geometry drift across
  a save. It is a cache of a measurement, not a decision.
- **Riders travel.** `stuckFollowers()` → `travelling()` (`layout.js:682`) →
  `startMove()` in `canvas/input.js:743`, the hover lift in `canvas/items.js`,
  `stuckPlacement()` for the Mobile layout, `attachRiders()`, and the stack
  order in `stacking.js:188`.

Two things follow immediately.

**Pinning is a decision, and decisions have to be stored.** "I unstuck this note"
cannot be re-derived from geometry, because the note may well still be lying on
the card it was unstuck from — that is the normal case, since you unstick it in
order to nudge it. So the change introduces the first piece of *durable*
stickiness state in the app: a per-item flag that means *this one is loose on
purpose*, and it has to survive a save, an undo, and a layout switch. The
header's argument still holds for the positive relation (which card is it on) and
we should not touch that; what is new is a negative override.

**"Immovable" has more than one door, and each one answers differently.** Pointer
drag (`startMove`) **redirects to the host**; arrow keys **unstick and nudge**;
`alignSelection` / `distributeSelection`, `rearrange` and the snap sweep
**skip** pinned items; the Mobile layout already skips them by treating them as
riders. Three behaviours across four doors is more than one rule, so the
predicate has to be shared even though the responses are not — see *How the
redirect is done*.

## Part 1 — Pinned notes

### The state

Add one durable field, on the item, not in a side table:

```js
item.meta.loose = true   // present only when the user explicitly unstuck it
```

Absent means the normal case. Present means: **do not measure, do not stick, do
not pin** — this thing is free, even though it is lying on something.

`sticky.js` grows a single guard at the top of `stuckTo()` and `wouldStick()`:
`if (note.meta?.loose) return null`. Everything downstream — riders, travel,
Mobile placement, stack order, `meta.stuckTo` stamping — then behaves exactly as
it does today for a note that happens to be over nothing. No new code paths.

### The gesture rules

| gesture | today | proposed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| drag a stuck note | moves the note off the card | **moves the host card**, note rides along |
| drag the host card | note travels | unchanged |
| resize the host | note rides the edge | unchanged |
| resize or rotate the note | free | **unchanged — pinning governs position only** |
| double-click a stuck note | opens the editor | **unchanged**; a pinned sticker still zooms |
| arrow keys on a stuck note | nudges it | **unsticks it and nudges** — the keyboard is the fine-positioning tool |
| right-click a stuck note | item menu | item menu **+ "Unstick"** |
| drag a loose note onto a card | sticks, stays draggable | **sticks and pins**; `meta.loose` is cleared by any drop that finds a host |
| right-click a loose note over a card | — | nothing new — dropping it on the card is the way back |

Because a drag on a pinned item **moves the host**, the pile reads as one object
under the pointer: press anywhere on the photo or on the star lying on it, and
the photo moves with the star riding along. That is already exactly what the
hover lift does today (`lastHoverId` → `travelling()` in `canvas/items.js`), so
the drag is being brought into line with the lift rather than given a new rule.

What still needs to be *legible* is that the star is not the thing moving. The
hover lift already lifts the whole pile, which says most of it; add a small pin
badge in the corner on hover for the rest. No toast — a message on every drag of
a stickered photo would be noise.

### How the redirect is done

One predicate, one place, so it cannot drift:

```js
// sticky.js — base layer, no state.js import
export const isPinned = it => !!stuckTo(it);       // loose flag already handled inside

/** The thing a press on `it` should actually take hold of: the top of its pile. */
export const dragRoot = it => { while (isPinned(it)) it = stuckTo(it); return it; };
```

`dragRoot()` walks up rather than one step, because a sticker on a note on a
photo has to move the photo. The walk terminates for the same reason
`stuckFollowers()` does — being stuck requires a lower z, so the relation is a
strict order.

Then:

- `canvas/input.js` `startMove(e, id)` — resolve `id` through `dragRoot()` before
  anything else, and drive the gesture from the root. **Selection is not
  changed**: clicking a pinned star still selects the star (so its own menu,
  colour and delete still work), it just drags the photo. Those two coming apart
  is the one genuinely new idea in this change and wants a comment saying so.
- A **multi-select** drag where one member is pinned to a non-member: map each
  driven id through `dragRoot()`, dedupe, then `travelling()` as today.
- the arrow-key handler in `input.js` — **neither**. An arrow press on a pinned
  item **clears `meta.loose`'s absence** — that is, sets `loose: true` — and then
  nudges the item itself. The keyboard is how you position a sticker precisely,
  and having ← move a photograph would be a very large effect from a very small
  key. Note what this leaves behind: the item stays loose afterwards, indefinitely,
  until the next *pointer* drag drops it on a host. That asymmetry is the design,
  not an oversight, and wants a comment saying so.
- `state.js` — the mutators that write geometry from a command rather than a
  gesture: `alignSelection`, `distributeSelection`, the arrangement apply, and
  the snap sweep. These want the filter applied at the door, not at each caller.
- `arrange/arrangements.js` already skips riders via `isRider()`; check it still
  does with the new flag in play.

### Deleting the host

**Stickers go with the card; notes stay behind.** Two rules rather than one, and
they are two because the things are two: a star on a photograph is a remark
about that photograph and means nothing once it is gone, while a note is
something you wrote and losing it to a delete you aimed at a picture would be
the app throwing away your words. Notes keep today's behaviour exactly — left on
the board, re-measured against whatever is underneath.

The cascade is one undo entry, so `Ctrl+Z` brings the photo and its stickers back
together. It follows hosts rather than flooding: a sticker on a *note* on a
deleted photo stays, because the note it is stuck to stays. The walk is
`stuckFollowers()` filtered to `type === 'sticker'` and cut short at the first
surviving host — worth a test, because the naive version takes the note's
stickers too.

### Migration — none

**Decided: old boards are repinned wholesale.** Every board anyone already has
opens with every note on a card suddenly immovable. There is no field to be
missing — absence means pinned — so nothing breaks and no migration code is
written; the boards simply behave differently after the update than before it.

The one consequence to accept knowingly: on an old board there is no way to tell
"stuck because the author meant it" from "stuck because it happened to overlap
the card's corner by 6%", and both now pin. `STICK_MIN` is deliberately low (5%)
because the old relation was cheap to escape — you just dragged the note away.
It is no longer cheap. Worth watching on a real board before shipping; if the
accidental pins are a nuisance, the lever is `STICK_MIN`, not a migration.

An older build opening a *new* board ignores `meta.loose` entirely and measures —
so an unstuck note lying on a card would be stuck again in the old build, and
still unstuck in the new one. That is an acceptable one-way degradation and is
the kind of thing `docs/mbrd-format.md` should say out loud.

## Part 2 — Stickers

### What a sticker *is*

**A board item of a new type, `'sticker'`.** Not a decoration stored in the
host's `meta`, and this is the load-bearing choice of the whole feature.

As an item it gets, for free: undo, selection, z-order, the trash, `.mbrd`
serialization, search (by name), the hover lift, culling, and — the point —
*every line of the stick machinery that Part 1 just rewrote*. As a field on the
host it would get none of those and would need a parallel implementation of all
of them.

The cost is that `sticker` shows up everywhere a type is switched on, which in
this codebase is a short list by design: a branch in `classify()` (not needed —
stickers never come from a file), an entry in `RENDERERS`, a `defaultSize`, and
whatever `ui/feed.js`, `ui/trash.js`, `ui/snapshot.js` and `ui/search.js` should
say about one.

```js
{ type: 'sticker', x, y, w, h, rot, z,
  name: 'Star',                       // what the trash and search show
  meta: { shape: 'star', tint: 3 } }  // + stuckTo, loose — as a note has
```

`w`/`h` square by default; `rot` free, because a star at 12° is the whole charm
of a sticker.

### Making them stick

`sticky.js` today says `note.type !== 'note'` in four places. Those become one
exported predicate:

```js
export const isSticky = it => it?.type === 'note' || it?.type === 'sticker';
```

and the same in `stacking.js:99`/`188`, `state.js:2304`, `layout.js`,
`import/drop.js`. Mechanical, but it must be *complete* — a sticker that sticks
in `stuckTo()` and is not recognised by `stuckFollowers()` is a sticker that gets
left behind when the card moves, which is the one bug this feature cannot have.
`tests/layers.test.js` and a new sticky test should pin the list down.

**What a sticker may stick to: everything.** Loose on bare paper, on a photo, on
a note, on a **fence**, and on the **title card**. The last two mean bending
`cannotHost()`, which today refuses both, so the exemption has to be a sticker
exemption and not a general loosening:

```js
const cannotHost = (it, rider) =>
  rider?.type === 'sticker' ? false : (isFurniture(it) || isFence(it));
```

The two reasons `cannotHost()` gives still have to be answered, not waved past:

- **A note on the fence around it would move twice.** It does not, in fact —
  `travelling()` dedupes by id — but the real hazard is Mobile, where a fence is
  a full-width band and a rider would ride the band instead of taking its place
  in the run. For a sticker that is the *desired* behaviour: a star on a fence's
  face is decoration on the region, and it should ride the band.
- **A note on the title card landed in the packed first row on Mobile as a rider
  nothing treated as an obstacle.** Same answer: a sticker is decoration and is
  not meant to be an obstacle. It rides, nothing packs around it, and that is
  correct rather than the bug it was for a note. `layout.js` parks the title card
  clear of the Mobile board, so a sticker on it goes with it.

**Fence membership**: a **loose** sticker inside a fence is a member and gets its
own cell when the fence is rearranged — it is an item standing in a region like
any other. A **pinned** one is a rider and is skipped, exactly as
`arrange/arrangements.js` skips riders today via `isRider()`. So the rule needs
no new code at all: it already falls out of `isRider()` once `isSticky()` covers
stickers. The thing to *test* is that it does, because "rearrange peeled every
star off its photo" is the failure mode.

### Drawing them

A new sprite, `web/assets/stickers.svg`, **not** `assets/icons.svg`.
`tests/icons.test.js` checks references against the sprite and the sprite against
the references in both directions; adding fifty shapes that no `<use href>` in
`index.html` mentions would fail it, and loosening that test to make room is a
worse trade than a second file. The sticker window's grid is generated from a
catalogue array (name, id, keywords, category), hand-written, `web/assets/js/
stickers/catalogue.js` — not generated, so unlike `import/formats.js` it is safe
to hand-edit.

`RENDERERS.sticker` returns an `<svg>` with a `<use href="assets/stickers.svg#s-star">`,
sized to the item box. **No `.card` wrapper**, so it gets none of the card
chrome, no cover slot: a sticker is a shape on a picture, and a shape in a
bordered box is a card with a star in it. `buildContent()`'s cover-attachment
test (`content.classList.contains('card')`) therefore skips it, which is correct
and already how `image` and `video` behave.

**Die-cut vinyl** is the house style (decided). Each shape is drawn twice from
one path: a fat `stroke` in the keyline colour under a `fill` in the tint, which
gives the white border a real die-cut sticker has for free and from a single
`<path>` per shape — no second sprite, no per-shape border geometry.

```html
<symbol id="s-star" viewBox="0 0 100 100">
  <path d="…" class="cut"/>   <!-- stroke: var(--sticker-cut); stroke-width: 9; -->
  <path d="…" class="ink"/>   <!-- fill:   var(--sticker-tint);                  -->
</symbol>
```

Plus a soft `filter: drop-shadow()` on the `<svg>` — a drop-shadow rather than a
box-shadow precisely because it follows the shape's outline and not its box, and
the shape is the whole point.

**Tilt: ±8°, re-rolled on every drop.** A sticker gets a fresh random angle each
time it lands somewhere, because that is what peeling one off and pressing it
down again looks like. Two consequences to hold: the roll must happen on *drop*
and not per frame, or the sticker will shimmer while you drag it; and undo has to
restore the previous angle, which it does for free as long as `rot` goes into the
same geometry snapshot as `x`/`y` (it already does — `GEOM_KEYS`). A sticker you
have rotated by hand still gets re-rolled if you move it to another card, which
is the one part of this that may want revisiting once it is in the hand.

**Size: fixed, 96×96 world units.** Not proportional to the host. A note is
120×120 by comparison, and a fixed size means the sticker you place is the
sticker you saw in the window — the alternative makes the same star come out
three different sizes on three different photos, and the resize handles are right
there for the case where that is wrong.

**Colour is a small tint palette**, sized and named like the note pad:
`--sticker-1..N` in `tokens.css`, `meta.tint` on the item, changed from the item
menu. `--sticker-cut` is the keyline and is one colour for all of them — a
die-cut border that changed with the tint would stop reading as the paper the
sticker is cut from.

**Every shape is tintable, and every shape has a default tint** carried in the
catalogue: the heart is born red, the star gold, the lightning bolt yellow. So
the palette is not cycled the way `addNote()` cycles `NOTE_TINTS` — a sticker
comes out of the window looking the way it looked *in* the window, and the
palette is an override rather than a lottery. The catalogue entry therefore
carries `{ id, name, tint }` and the tints have to be chosen so that every shape
is legible in all of them, since any shape can be set to any tint.

Styling lives in a new block in `web/assets/css/items.css` — no new stylesheet,
so the order documented in `docs/architecture.md` is untouched.

### The sticker window

Model it on the Playlist's Desktop window (`ui/playlist.js`), and register it
with `ui/panel-stack.js` so it takes its turn on the right edge instead of
fighting the player and the header bar for it.

- Opened by a toolbar button (`<button data-cmd="stickers">` in `index.html`) and
  a `cmds.stickers` entry in `commands.js` — the toolbar/command route is the
  documented one for a new user-facing action.
- Body: a scrolling grid of shape tiles under category headings — at ~40 shapes
  no filter field is needed, and adding one later is cheap.
- Draggable and resizable title bar, close button, remembers its position while
  the app is open.
- **Favourites**, and they are **app-wide, not per board**: a star on a tile
  keeps it in a pinned row at the top, and the list follows you from board to
  board. That means it is *not* a `.mbrd` key — it goes in the same local store
  the app's other preferences use, so a board you send someone carries your
  stickers and not your picks. Check where `storage/session.js` and
  `storage/library.js` already put app-level state and use that door; a new
  top-level board key for this would be the wrong shape.
- **Remembers its last category** across opens, the way the Playlist window
  remembers player-vs-feed. A module-scope variable, not persisted.
- No recently-used row and no keyboard shortcut, deliberately: favourites cover
  the same need with less machinery, and the letter keys are worth keeping free.

### Getting a sticker from the window onto a card

The canvas is entirely pointer-events; the only HTML5 drag-and-drop in the app is
`import/drop.js`, for files arriving from outside. Both gestures ship:

- **Pointer drag with a ghost** (Desktop). `pointerdown` on a tile captures the
  pointer, clones the shape into a fixed-position element that follows it, and on
  `pointerup` over the canvas converts through `vp.toWorld()` and calls
  `addSticker(shape, centre)`. While dragging, run the same `wouldStick()` +
  `showStickTarget()` highlight the note drag already has, so you can see which
  card it will land on before you let go.
- **Tap to arm** (both, and the only thing that works on a phone). Tap a shape,
  tap a card, it lands on it. The armed shape wants a visible state — the tile
  held down, and the cursor carrying the shape — or a board that places a star on
  the next click is a board that has been possessed.

A sticker dropped on empty board is loose. A sticker dropped on a card is stuck
and — by Part 1 — pinned immediately. Which is the intended feel, and also the
reason Part 1 must land first.

**Mobile placement is in scope**, and it is the one part of this plan with no
existing path to copy. The Mobile layout is a native-scrolling DOM feed
(`ui/feed.js`), not the world-space canvas, so a tap on a photo there has to be
turned into a *world* position on that item before `addSticker()` can be called —
the tap point as a fraction of the tile, then that fraction of the item's box.
Nothing today converts feed coordinates back to world coordinates. It is a small
function but it is new, and it is the piece most likely to be underestimated;
`arrange/feed-layout.js` and `stuckPlacement()` are where the fraction maths
already lives.

### The shapes

**~40 in the first pass** (decided), grouped — the window can then show them as
one scrolling grid with category headings and no filter field, which it would
need past about sixty. A first cut:

- **Marks** star, five-point star outline, burst/sparkle, heart, broken heart,
  check, cross, plus, asterisk, dot
- **Punctuation** exclamation, double exclamation, question, interrobang, ellipsis
- **Arrows** straight, curved, corner, double, circular
- **Flags & pins** pin, pushpin, flag, ribbon, bookmark, tag
- **Faces & hands** smile, frown, wink, thumbs-up, thumbs-down, ok
- **Talk** speech bubble, thought bubble, banner
- **Weather & bits** lightning, flame, cloud, sun, moon, crown, skull, gem, leaf

Every one of them is a single `<path>` in a 100×100 viewBox, drawn twice (see
above). Forty hand-authored paths is the largest single lump of work in this
plan and the one thing that cannot be hurried; the catalogue is deliberately a
plain array so shapes can land a handful at a time.

## Staging

1. **`meta.loose` + pin enforcement**, notes only. No new type, no window. This
   is the change that alters existing boards, and it should be able to be shipped
   and reverted on its own. Menu entry "Unstick" lands here.
2. **`isSticky()` sweep** — the predicate replaces the `type === 'note'` tests in
   `sticky.js`, `stacking.js`, `layout.js`, `state.js`. Still no new type; pure
   refactor, tests unchanged and green.
3. **The `sticker` type** — catalogue, sprite, `RENDERERS` entry, `defaultSize`,
   `addSticker()` in `import/drop.js` next to `addNote()`, CSS. Placed only from
   the console (`mbrd.cmds`) to start; no UI.
4. **The window** — panel, grid, category headings, favourites, pointer drag and
   tap-to-arm, `data-cmd`, `panel-stack` registration, toolbar icon.
5. **Mobile** — the feed's tap-to-place path and the feed-to-world coordinate
   conversion, plus drawing stickers on their hosts' tiles. The only genuinely
   new mechanism in the plan, and the reason it is last rather than folded into 4.
6. **The trimmings** — tint picker in the item menu, `ui/snapshot.js` branch,
   trash entry, `docs/mbrd-format.md` and `docs/architecture.md` updates,
   `web/sw.js` `SHELL` additions for the new sprite and modules (remember: no
   apostrophes anywhere in that array).

Each of 1–5 is a `save.bat` version on its own.

## Things that will bite

- **`web/sw.js` `SHELL`** needs the new sprite, the catalogue and the window
  module, or the app is broken offline in a way nothing tests catch until a
  plane. Call it out in the commit — it is a service-worker cache change.
- **`.mbrd` schema change**: a new `type`, a new `meta.shape`/`meta.tint`, a new
  `meta.loose`. Both `docs/mbrd-format.md` and the "call it out explicitly" rule
  in `CLAUDE.md` apply.
- **An older build opening a board with stickers** falls through to
  `RENDERERS.generic` — a plain grey card with a name on it, in the sticker's
  place. Honest, but worth knowing; there is no version gate in the format.
- **Hit-testing stays rectangular**, like every other item — the whole box
  selects the sticker. Decided, and it is the conservative choice: a loose
  sticker stays easy to grab by its corner. The cost is a dead zone over the
  picture around every big star, and the escape hatch if it grates is one CSS
  line (`pointer-events: visiblePainted` on the path, transparent parent).
- **Connections** (`canvas/web.js`, and the in-flight `ui/conn-chip.js`) — a
  sticker as an endpoint is almost certainly wrong, and `isContent()` in
  `board-model.js` will happily say a sticker is content. Needs an explicit no —
  and note this is the *same* `isContent()` that decides fence membership, where
  a loose sticker *is* meant to count. Two different questions, one predicate:
  check before reusing it, or the exclusion for connections will silently take
  stickers out of fences too.
- **The Mobile feed** (`ui/feed.js`) must draw a pinned sticker **on its host's
  tile**, at the fraction of the host it holds on the canvas — the same
  `stuckPlacement()` maths the Mobile layout already uses for notes. The board
  you made is the board you see. Loose stickers in the feed are the open edge
  here (a wall tile containing one star is odd); starting by drawing only the
  pinned ones is the safe read.
- **`ui/snapshot.js`** paints the board to a canvas for the board thumbnail and
  needs a sticker branch, or a board covered in stars looks bare in the board
  list. It draws with `fillStyle` and primitives, so a sticker means rasterising
  an SVG path — the least pleasant thirty lines in this plan.
- **`ui/trash.js`** gets a sticker branch too: deleted stickers go to the bin
  like anything else, with the shape as the thumbnail.

## Decisions

Settled 2026-08-12, in conversation. Where a decision has a consequence the plan
had to absorb, it is named here and argued where it lands above.

**Pinning**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Drag a pinned item | **Moves its host**, whole pile as one object. Selection still lands on the item itself. |
| Existing boards | **Repinned wholesale.** No migration, no schema bump. `STICK_MIN` is the lever if accidental pins grate. |
| Drop a loose item on a card | **Re-pins automatically.** `meta.loose` is cleared by any drop that finds a host. |
| "Stick to this card" menu entry | **No.** Dropping it on the card is the way back. |
| Arrow keys | **Unstick and nudge.** The item stays loose until the next pointer drop. |
| Double-click | **Unchanged** — pinned notes still open the editor, pinned stickers still zoom. |
| Resize / rotate a pinned item | **Allowed.** Pinning governs position only. |
| Delete the host card | **Stickers go with it; notes stay behind.** One undo entry. |

**Stickers**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| What it is | A board item, `type: 'sticker'` — not a field on the host. |
| Artwork | **Die-cut vinyl**: keyline, drop shadow, tilt. ~40 shapes in the first pass. |
| Colour | **Small tint palette**, every shape tintable, each with a **default tint** in the catalogue. |
| Size | **Fixed, 96×96** world units. Not proportional to the host. |
| Tilt | **±8°, re-rolled on every drop.** |
| Hosts | Everything — bare board, cards, notes, **fences**, the **title card**. `cannotHost()` gets a sticker exemption. |
| Fence membership | **Loose** stickers are members and get a cell on Rearrange; **pinned** ones ride their host. Falls out of `isRider()`. |
| Hit area | **Rectangular**, like every other item. |
| Placement | Pointer-drag with a ghost on Desktop, **tap-to-arm on both**. |
| Mobile | **In scope.** Tap a shape, tap a card in the feed. Needs a new feed→world coordinate step. |
| Feed | Stickers **drawn on their host's tile**, at the fraction they hold on the canvas. |
| Snapshot, Trash | **Both get a branch.** Search does not. |
| Window extras | **Favourites** (app-wide, *not* in the `.mbrd`) and **remembers its last category**. No recents row, no keyboard shortcut. |
| Text stickers | **Not in v1.** Bubbles and banners exist as decoration; a shape you type into is its own feature. |

## Still open

Small things, none of them blocking — most will answer themselves in the hand.

1. **Loose stickers in the Mobile feed.** Pinned ones draw on their host's tile.
   A loose one has no tile to draw on, and a wall tile containing one star is
   odd. Starting by simply not drawing them is the safe read, and it is the one
   place where the board you made is not quite the board you see.
2. **The pin badge.** A small pin in the corner on hover is the plan; whether it
   reads as *pinned* or as *drag handle* is a thing to look at rather than argue
   about, and if the hover lift already says it, the badge can go.
3. **Re-rolling tilt on a hand-rotated sticker.** If you have deliberately turned
   a star to 45° and then move it to another photo, the re-roll throws your angle
   away. The fix, if it grates, is to re-roll only if `rot` is still the angle it
   was born with — cheap, but it means remembering that angle.
4. **The tint set.** Any shape can take any tint, so the palette has to be
   legible on all forty shapes at once. Picking it wants the sprite to exist
   first.
5. **`isContent()` is doing two jobs** — connection eligibility and fence
   membership — and stickers want opposite answers from it. Splitting it is
   probably right; doing so is a change to a base layer and wants its own look.
6. **Where app-wide preferences actually live.** Favourites need a local store
   that is not the board file. `storage/session.js` and `storage/library.js` are
   the candidates; neither is obviously the right door yet.
