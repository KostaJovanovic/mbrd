# Pan/zoom performance — root causes and plan (2026-07-30)

## Status (implemented 2026-07-30)

Shipped on `main`, unit-tested (595 pass), **not yet verified on the iPhone**:

- **C (instrumentation)** — `report()` median-NaN fixed; the cull hot path is now
  timed (`cullProfile`); the HUD/`report()` show live mounted-node, video and
  decoded-image-MB counts (`viewStats`). Arm with `#perf`. `d81cce2`-adjacent /
  earlier commits.
- **A1 (display-resolution mount)** — `canvas/display.js`: photos mount a
  card-sized (1280px) WebP copy made once per session, serialized; originals kept
  for export/optimize. The primary crash fix for photo boards. `d81cce2`.
- **A2 (release decode on discard)** — `discard()` clears `<img>` src. `398c90d`.
- **A3 (video decoders capped on touch)** — clips mount source-less on touch,
  source attached on first play; parked clips hold no decoder. `88dcde8`.
- **B2 (shadow twins dropped at far zoom)** — `#world.zoom-far .item-shadow`.
  `398c90d`.

Decisions taken (see "Open questions"): A1 = **derived + session-cached** (no
`.mbrd` change); A3 = **touch only** (desktop unchanged); MIN_ZOOM = **kept at
0.1** (no fit-floor).

**Still open:** B1 (throttle cull during motion — the plan's skip-detach variant
was rejected as it can grow memory unbounded during a sustained pan; a
throttle-not-skip form is TODO), A4 (LOD proxy), A5 (memory-budget net), B3
(skip grid on sub-pixel deltas), B4 (note-toolbar reflow). These are jank-tuning
and should be driven by the on-device `#perf` baseline now that the crash fixes
are in — measure which lever the frame actually spends time in before cutting.

---

Populated boards pan and zoom badly with fewer than 50 items, and on an iPhone,
zooming out to frame the whole board **crashes Safari** at that same small item
count. This document is the research behind both symptoms and a prioritised plan
to fix them. It supersedes and extends
`research/2026-07-29-grid-perf-handoff.md` (the grid tier-fade skip, the
mobile-var skip and the `mbrd.perf` profiler described there still stand and are
assumed in place).

The two symptoms have **different causes** and must not be conflated:

- **The crash is a memory/decoder ceiling**, not a slow frame. It is caused by
  *what gets mounted and held*, not by how long a frame takes.
- **The jank is per-frame CPU and compositor cost**, caused by *how much work
  every view frame does* and *how many layers the compositor carries*.

Fixing the jank will not stop the crash, and vice versa. They are sequenced
below with the crash first, because a crash on a 50-item board on the target
device is the more serious defect.

---

## Part A — The crash: memory ceiling on zoom-out

### A. Root cause

Three code facts compound into one failure:

1. **Zoom-out mounts the entire board at once.** `sync()`
   (`web/assets/js/canvas/items.js:330-376`) mounts every item whose box
   intersects `vp.visibleRect(cullMargin())`. `visibleRect`'s half-extent is
   `cx / zoom` (`web/assets/js/canvas/viewport.js:370-374`), so at `MIN_ZOOM = 0.1`
   (`viewport.js:25`) the mounted rectangle is ~10× the screen in world units —
   i.e. *every item on the board becomes "visible."* `BUILD_BUDGET = 12`
   (`items.js:280`) only spreads the builds across frames; the steady state is
   all cards mounted. Culling frees memory only for items that *leave* the
   viewport — and when the whole board is framed, nothing leaves.

2. **Each photo holds a full-resolution decoded bitmap that is never released.**
   The image renderer sets the full-res original as `img.src`
   (`web/assets/js/canvas/renderers.js:364-370`); import stores and mounts the
   **native-resolution original** (`web/assets/js/import/drop.js:457`), with no
   pixel downscale — `optimize/` is a manual menu action, not an import step
   (`web/assets/js/optimize/optimize.js:1-8`). A 100px thumbnail twin is added
   only when `item.meta.thumb` exists, and the far-zoom "swap" is **CSS
   `display:none` only** (`web/assets/css/app.css:967-969`): it never clears the
   full-res `src` and never revokes the object URL
   (`web/assets/js/storage/assets.js:17-22` creates URLs once and holds them for
   the session; `items.js discard()` at `items.js:194-204` clears `<video>`/
   `<audio>` src but **not** `<img>`). A hidden `<img>` in Safari keeps its
   decoded bitmap. A 6000×4000 phone photo is ~96 MB decoded; a handful of them
   held live is already a crash budget. Images without a thumb render full-res
   *and stay displayed* at far zoom.

3. **Each video is a live decoder with no cap.** Every mounted video is a real
   `<video preload="metadata">` with `src = url + '#t=0.1'`
   (`renderers.js:439-441`), and the `#t=0.1` fragment forces a first-frame
   decode — exactly the resource iOS Safari rations to a small number of
   simultaneous decoders. Nothing in the code limits how many `<video>` elements
   are live at once (`web/assets/js/canvas/video.js`, `canvas/audio.js` only pause
   *other playing* clips, they do not reduce element/decoder count). Audio adds a
   full PCM decode per file for the waveform (`audio.js:228-297`), deduped per
   hash but transiently large.

**Net:** framing the whole board mounts N full-resolution bitmaps + N video
decoders + N multi-layer shadow twins simultaneously, with no release path while
they stay on screen. On iOS that exceeds the decoded-image / video-decoder /
total-tab memory budget and the tab is killed. `#world` being a single
`will-change: transform` layer scaled to `0.1` over the whole-board extent
(`app.css:103`, `viewport.js:712`) is a plausible *secondary* amplifier (one
large tiled layer), but the decoded-media story is the primary and most certain
cause.

### A. Fixes, in priority order

**A1 — Mount at display resolution, not native resolution. (Biggest lever.)**
No card is ever shown larger than a few hundred CSS px, yet the app decodes the
full original. Generate a bounded display-resolution variant and mount *that*;
keep the original bytes only for export/optimize.

- Preferred: on import, alongside the existing 100px `thumb`, derive a
  mid-resolution display asset (e.g. long edge ~1280–1600px, WebP) and set
  `img.src` to it; load the original only when a card is both selected/opened and
  large on screen. This reuses the existing `shrinkPicture()` machinery
  (`optimize/picture.js`, `MAX_SIDE = 1200`) but runs it at import in the worker
  (`optimize/media-worker.js`) instead of only from the menu.
- Cheaper interim: at far zoom (`#world.is-stilled`), swap the *actual `src`* to
  the thumbnail rather than toggling `display`, and restore the full `src` when
  the card grows back. This releases the full-res decode instead of merely
  hiding it. Requires clearing `src` in JS, not CSS.
- Decision needed: automatic import downscale changes stored data and the
  `optimize` story. See "Open questions." A display variant that is *derived and
  not persisted* (regenerated on load) avoids the `.mbrd` schema question
  entirely.

**A2 — Release decoded memory on detach and at far zoom.**
Extend `discard()` (`items.js:194-204`) to also clear `<img>` `src` (and
`.still` twins) when a node is truly discarded, so GC can reclaim the decode
promptly instead of waiting on node collection. Independently, add a far-zoom
release: below `stillZoom()`, null out full-res `<img>` `src` for off-thumbnail
images. Pair with A4 so the card still shows *something* (the thumb) when its
full-res src is gone.

**A3 — Cap simultaneous video decoders.**
Do not mount videos as live `<video>` by default. Mount a poster/first-frame
still (the app already extracts a poster for undecodable clips — generalise it to
all clips at import) and instantiate the real `<video>` element only on demand:
on tap-to-play, or when the card is large on screen and the pointer is near it.
Pool a small fixed number of `<video>` elements (e.g. 2–3) and recycle the
least-recently-used. This is the single change most likely to stop the crash on
video-heavy boards, and it mirrors the iOS decoder model directly.

**A4 — Level-of-detail (LOD) proxy at far zoom. (Durable scalability fix.)**
Below a zoom threshold, stop mounting heavy media DOM entirely and draw a cheap
proxy: the thumbnail `<img>` (already tiny) or, at extreme zoom-out, a single
canvas blit of coloured rectangles keyed to each item's dominant colour. This
bounds memory and layer count by *screen area*, not by *item count*, and is the
only fix that makes "see the whole board" safe regardless of how large the board
grows. `stillZoom()` (0.4 mouse / 0.55 touch, `viewport.js:183-194`) and the
existing `#world.is-stilled` / `zoom-far` machinery give a natural hook.

**A5 — Hard memory budget with graceful degradation.**
Track an estimate of live decoded bytes (sum of mounted `naturalWidth ×
naturalHeight × 4`, plus live video count). When it crosses a device-aware
ceiling, force everything past the nearest items down to thumbnails/proxies even
if not yet at far zoom. A safety net so a pathological board degrades instead of
dying. Lower priority; do after A1–A4 and measure whether it is still needed.

## Part B — The jank: per-frame cost on zoom-out

### B. Root cause

Every emitted `'change'` (one per painted view frame) synchronously runs 8
permanent listeners plus 1 conditional (`viewport.js:776`, dispatch is a plain
loop in `util.js:110-114`). On **zoom-out specifically** the two heaviest do
their maximum work every frame:

1. **`items.js` re-culls every frame.** `syncView()` (`items.js:307-320`)
   early-returns while the visible rect stays inside the padded `syncedRect` — a
   guarantee that holds when zooming *in* (rect shrinks) but breaks immediately
   when zooming *out* (rect grows past the pad). So zoom-out falls through to a
   full `sync(false)` — spatial query + mount loop + detach loop over all
   mounted nodes — **on every frame**, with no `vp.moving` guard. (Documented
   asymmetry: `items.js:296-299`.)

2. **`--iz` write invalidates every card each frame.** `_paint()` writes the
   inherited `--iz` custom property (`viewport.js:718`, `_setIz` at
   `viewport.js:661-665`); because `#world` inherits it to every card, the
   browser re-resolves chrome sizing on every card and its children each frame.
   The file's own comment calls this "the single largest reason zooming a full
   board feels heavy" (`viewport.js:63-82`). It is already quantised to 8% steps
   during motion (`izRung`) — verify that mitigation is actually landing under
   the profiler.

3. **Grid repaints full-viewport every frame** (`main.js:407-417` →
   `grid.js:181-303`): 2–8 CSS radial gradients re-parsed and string-compared,
   `backgroundPosition` rewritten every frame (`grid.js:288-301`); on the Harsh
   tier, a full canvas `clearRect` + 2–4 `Path2D` fills of thousands of rects
   (`grid.js:672-694`, `lattice()` `grid.js:721-785`).

4. **Shadow twins are not dropped at far zoom.** Card chrome (labels, bars,
   grips, waveforms) is dropped at far zoom, but there is **no**
   `#world.zoom-far .item-shadow { display:none }` rule — so a full-board
   zoom-out composites one multi-layer `box-shadow` element per item
   (`app.css:120-137`, 2-layer default / 3-layer notes).

5. **A note being edited forces a synchronous reflow every frame**
   (`notes.js:383-387`: `getBoundingClientRect` ×2 + `offsetWidth/Height`), via
   the conditional listener `notes.js:399`.

### B. Fixes, in priority order

**B1 — Throttle `sync()` during live motion.** During `vp.moving`, do a
mount-only pass (add newly-visible nodes so nothing pops in) and **skip the
detach pass and any restack**; run the full `sync(false)` once on settle (the
`_moving()` trailing timer already emits a second `'change'` on settle,
`viewport.js:656`, so the hook exists). This gives the same coverage as stepped
zoom without the UX cost of discrete jumps, and removes the per-frame detach
walk. This directly answers the earlier "should we step zoom on mobile?"
question: **no — throttle instead; stepping treats the symptom and hurts the
gesture.**

**B2 — Drop shadow twins at far zoom.** Add
`#world.zoom-far .item-shadow { display: none }` (mirroring the chrome-drop
rules). At far zoom the shadows are invisible anyway and each is a multi-layer
composited element. Trivial change, real compositor saving on the exact
crash/jank case; also helps Part A (fewer layers).

**B3 — Skip grid repaint on negligible view deltas.** When a view change moves
the grid less than ~1 device px (sub-pixel pans, micro-zoom), skip `paintGrid`.
Guard cheaply before the gradient string-build.

**B4 — Cache the note toolbar rect.** Cache the measurements in `notes.js` and
recompute only on resize/scroll, not per view frame, to remove the
edit-time forced reflow (`notes.js:383-387`).

**B5 — Reduce gradient count / batch listener work.** Lower priority, confirm
with the profiler first: cut the steady-state gradient layers, or coalesce the
cheaper listeners. Do only if the baseline says the grid or fan-out is still hot
after B1–B4.

## Part C — Instrumentation (do first, it gates everything)

The profiler (`mbrd.perf`, `main.js`) currently has blind spots that would let
any fix look better or worse than it is.

**C1 — Fix the `report()` median-NaN bug.** `report()` destructures
`{ sorted, janks }` but uses a bare `median` (`main.js:369,372`) → `fpsMedian`
is always `NaN`. Change to `const { sorted, median, janks } = stats();`. The HUD
is unaffected; only the console headline is wrong. (Already flagged in the
2026-07-29 handoff.)

**C2 — Measure item sync.** The profiler wraps `paintGrid` + `afterGrid` but not
`items.js syncView/sync`, which is the actual zoom-out hot path. Add a timing
hook so sync cost is visible.

**C3 — Add a memory readout.** Count mounted nodes, live `<video>` elements, and
an estimate of decoded image bytes (Σ `naturalWidth × naturalHeight × 4`), shown
on the HUD. Without this, the crash work is guesswork; with it, "zoom out, watch
the number climb, watch it plateau after A1–A4" is a direct proof.

## Sequencing

1. **C1–C3** — trustworthy numbers and a memory readout (small, do first).
2. **Baseline** on the iPhone that crashes and on desktop: capture median/p95
   fps and the decoded-bytes/live-video counts under sustained zoom-out on a
   ~50-item board (mix of photos and videos). Confirm which of A1/A3 dominates
   the crash.
3. **A3** (video decoder cap) and **A2** (release on detach / far zoom) — likely
   the fastest path to *stop the crash*, low schema risk.
4. **A1** (display-resolution mount) — the durable memory fix; decide
   persisted-vs-derived first (see below).
5. **B1** (throttle sync during motion) and **B2** (drop shadows at far zoom) —
   the biggest jank wins, both low risk.
6. **A4** (LOD proxy) — the scalability fix; do once the crash is contained and
   measure whether it is still needed for very large boards.
7. **B3/B4/B5, A5** — polish and safety net, driven by the profiler.

Re-baseline after each step. Treat every "this should help" as a hypothesis
until the profiler and the memory readout confirm it.

## Open questions / decisions for the user

- **Display variant: persisted or derived?** Persisting a mid-res variant in the
  `.mbrd` changes the format (`docs/mbrd-format.md`) and interacts with the
  manual `optimize` step and `meta.was` undo. Deriving it on load (not stored)
  avoids the schema question but costs a decode+encode at open time. Deriving is
  the lower-risk default; confirm before A1.
- **Video default:** is mounting videos as a still-until-tapped acceptable UX?
  It changes first-frame liveliness on desktop, where the crash is not a problem.
  Could be gated to touch/mobile, or applied everywhere for consistency.
- **How far should zoom-out go?** `MIN_ZOOM = 0.1`. If the real need is only
  "fit the whole board," `zoom-to-fit` never needs arbitrary zoom-out; a tighter
  `MIN_ZOOM` plus a fit button may remove the worst case cheaply.
